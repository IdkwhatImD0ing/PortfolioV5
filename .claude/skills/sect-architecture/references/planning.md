# Sect planning reference

Long version of the `sect-architecture` skill. Section A to E are design rules with sources.
Section F lists checks the walkcheck script does not run yet. Section G is a connection plan for
each Ming Sect area. Section H is the full phrase bank. Section I is every source.

Bracketed numbers like [3] point to the source list in section I.

## A. How a temple or palace complex stays connected

- **The axis is a route, not a view line.** It is built on and moved along through a series of
  gates and halls. The main gate, the centre door of every axial hall and the stairs to each
  hall sit on one straight line. [1][2]
- **Fixed order, front to back.** Buddhist: spirit screen or paifang archway, often a release
  pond crossed by a bridge on the axis, the mountain gate (shanmen), bell tower and drum tower
  just inside, the Hall of Heavenly Kings, the main (Mahavira) hall, then the Dharma hall and
  sutra library at the back. Taoist: gate, guardian hall (Lingguan or Dragon-and-Tiger hall),
  main hall, rear halls such as a Parents' Hall. [6][7][26][23]
- **Bell and drum towers.** The bell tower stands east, the drum tower west (左钟右鼓: left and
  right as seen from the main hall looking out). They are a mirrored pair. [7]
- **Importance grows the further in you go.** The main hall is the biggest building, on the
  highest platform, with the most bays and the grandest roof. Gates and front halls are smaller.
  Rear halls and living quarters are smaller and quieter. [29][5]
- **Courtyard units (jin).** Each courtyard is closed front and back by a hall or wall with a
  gate. You reach the next one through a hall that has a front and a back door on the axis, or
  through small passage doors beside the hall. A hall with no rear exit is a dead end that cuts
  off everything behind it. [9][13]
- **Side halls face the courtyard.** Matched pairs face each other across it, doors toward the
  axis, never toward the outer wall. [8][7]
- **A raised paved walkway (yongdao)** runs down the middle of each courtyard on the axis, from
  the gate to the foot of the next hall's stairs. [13][23]
- **Every hall stands on a raised platform (taiji)** with a flight of steps (tadu) in front of the
  centre door, about as wide as the centre bay. Larger halls add a moon terrace (yuetai) at
  platform height, with three flights at its front and one at each side. [13][14][18]
- **Stair type matches rank.** Top: a carved stone ramp up the middle, steps either side (people
  use the side steps). Middle: steps with sloped edge slabs (chuidai). Minor: plain steps you can
  climb from three sides (ruyi). Long or high flights get balustrades. [15][14][2]
- **Platform height and step count rise with rank.** A standard Qing platform is about 15% of
  the eave-column height. The top hall can sit on stacked terraces (the Hall of Supreme Harmony
  has three tiers). Step counts are usually odd. So an ordinary hall sits on a knee-to-waist base
  with a short flight. [17][2][16]
- **The complex is walled; gates are the only way in.** The main gate has three openings: a large
  one on the axis and two smaller ones beside it. Big complexes add side gates beside the main
  gate and gates in the east, west and north walls, each meeting a path. [11][3][34]
- **The spirit screen (zhaobi)** faces the main gate on the axis, outside across the approach or
  just inside. The path goes round it on both sides. [12][10]
- **Covered corridors (huilang)** ring the courtyard, join the gate to the side halls and the side
  halls to the main hall. Each touches a building at both ends, at that floor level. Tiantong
  Temple's halls are joined so that there is "no sun on fine days, no wet paths on rainy days".
  [19][20][34][22]
- **Corridors between levels** step down in level stages (dieluo lang) or climb the slope
  (pashan lang). The Summer Palace's Hall of Dispelling Clouds and Hall of Virtuous Splendor are
  joined this way. [25][21]
- **One long gallery can tie scattered buildings together.** The Summer Palace Long Corridor runs
  728 m along the shore with four pavilions. The gate of the hillside axis sits at its midpoint,
  so the cross axis and the long path meet at one gate. [24]

## B. Mountain routes

- **On a mountain, the climbing road is the axis.** Depth becomes height. Buildings string along
  the ascent the way courtyards string along a flat axis. The summit holds the most sacred hall.
  [29]
- **Terrace the slope.** Each courtyard is a level terrace held by a retaining wall, joined to the
  next by stairs on the axis. Purple Heaven Palace on Wudang has five rising terraces with a hall
  on each. Rooflines stack up the hill because the buildings rise
  with the slope. [26][27][31][38]
- **Stairways are built works:** retaining walls, drainage, bridges over gullies, landings to
  rest. Broad dressed flights lead to major halls; narrow rough ones to minor sites. Gateways and
  arches stand across the stair at thresholds. [30]
- **Mark the climb with gates in order.** Mount Tai: Daizong archway, First Gate of Heaven, Red
  Gate, Midway Gate of Heaven, Dragon Gate archway, Ascending-Immortal archway, South Gate of
  Heaven, Heavenly Street, Azure Cloud Temple, summit. About 11 gates, 14 archways and 14
  pavilions over 9 to 10 km. [35][36][32]
- **Split steep stairs into stages.** Mount Tai's route breaks into segments of about 50 to 550 m,
  each ending at a gate, bridge, pavilion or archway. The Eighteen Bends climbs about 400 m in
  under 1 km on about 1,600 steps, in three stages (slow, medium, steep). [32][37]
- **Put the top gate in the saddle.** The South Gate of Heaven stands at the low point between two
  peaks, so the stair aims straight at it and the peaks frame it. [36]
- **Tie the lower temple to the mountain axis.** Dai Temple sits on the same line that runs to the
  South Gate of Heaven; its back gate opens onto the start of the mountain path. [34][33]
- **Space stops by walking effort.** On Mount Emei, temples on the pilgrim routes are at most
  about 7.5 km apart, each with food and lodging; small pavilions fill the gaps. [39]
- **Branch off the main path.** Where the path is too narrow, the temple sits on flatter ground
  beside it, reached by a short side stair; its gate faces that stair (Leiyin Temple). [39]
- **Stairs can pass through gate buildings.** Big steps lead to the gate, and a further stair
  continues inside up to the main hall (Chunyang Temple). [39]
- **Site like an armchair.** A peak behind, ridges at both sides, open ground and water in front.
  Halls face the open, downhill side. On mountains that can be east or west (the Hanging Temple
  faces east, Jinshan Temple west). [31][39][41][52]
- **The summit group is small but walled.** The largest complexes sit low where there is room.
  The Wudang summit is ringed by a 345 m stone wall with four gates, reached by steep stairs.
  [26][29]

## C. Cliffs, plank roads and bridges

- **Cliff halls (Hanging Temple):** horizontal timber beams socketed into the rock (27
  oil-soaked beams), beam ends sticking out about 1 m past the building to carry a walkway. The
  building sits in a natural recess under an overhang, with a channel cut above to shed rock and
  water. The posts underneath are mostly for show. [41][43]
- **Hanging Temple circulation, south to north:** gate at the south end tight against the cliff,
  a compact courtyard group (bell and drum towers, main hall, side halls), then two three-storey
  towers with corridors on their open sides and narrow stairs inside. A plank walkway joins their
  top floors and a rock-cut path joins their bottom floors. Walkways are one person wide. All
  about 40 rooms are reachable. [41][42]
- **Plank roads (zhandao):** a row of holes cut in the cliff, beams pushed in, planks on the
  beams, chains or railings on the open side. Posts come up from below where the gap is wide.
  Both ends join stone paths or bridges. [45][46][47]
- **Bridges sit on the axis in front of gates.** Five bridges cross the Golden Water River in
  front of the Gate of Supreme Harmony. Temples cross the release pond on a central bridge
  between the archway and the mountain gate. The bridge lines up with the gate opening. [2][4][23]
- **Covered bridges (langqiao):** a roofed timber deck with benches along both sides, often a
  shrine, sometimes an upper loft. A bridge can carry a small building, like the 10 m bridge
  between the Hanging Temple's towers with a shrine on it. [48][49][44]
- **Chain bridges:** Luding Bridge has 13 chains (9 under the deck, 4 as handrails), a span of
  about 100 m, chains anchored in piers on both banks, and wooden bridge houses at each end. A
  visible bridgehead tells the eye the bridge is finished. [73][74]
- **Huashan:** the plank walk uses holes chiselled into the cliff, wedged posts, planks and iron
  chains. The Qianchi stair is about 300 steep steps with chains on both sides. [75][76]
- **Pavilions are rest stops on paths:** roadside pavilions about every 10 li (about 5 km),
  halfway-up pavilions on mountain stairs, and pavilions at viewpoints and junctions. A pavilion
  always sits on or beside the path. [50][51][53]

## D. Reading rank from outside

Roof: hipped outranks hip-and-gable, which outranks gabled. Double eaves outrank single. Glazed
tile outranks grey. More bays (odd: 3, 5, 7, 9) means higher rank. Rank also shows in platform
height, number of steps, stone balustrades, bracket sets, and how many gates you passed to get
there. So the summit hall gets double eaves and the biggest platform; huts and side halls get
simple roofs and short steps. [29][17]

## E. How the genre lays out a sect

- **Route first.** Moonlight Blade's scene lead designs the walking path first, places buildings
  along it, then judges the view from every point on it. [54]
- **Outer to core by height and distance.** Outer disciples near the mountain gate (lowest,
  busiest, plainest). Inner peaks higher and further. Elders and the sect master on the highest
  or most secluded peaks. The core (ancestral hall, treasury, formation core) is hardest to
  reach. Some novels make this four nested formation rings. [67][68][69]
- **Hub and spoke.** Zhu Xian's Qingyun Sect has Tongtian Peak in the centre and six more peaks.
  In Renegade Immortal ch.2 a silver crescent bridge runs from the main hall's peak to another
  peak. Link each side peak to the main peak, not only to its neighbours. [64][65][57]
- **One spine.** Mountain gate at the bottom, one long stone stair broken by landings and small
  gate arches, a big plaza at the top, the main hall on the axis above it. Renegade Immortal ch.2
  opens on a twisting stair down the mountain from the hall; the Heng Yue entrance test is
  walking up those steps. [57][58][72][78][79]
- **Formal halls on the axis, daily life to the sides.** Each formal hall sits on a terrace
  higher than the one before; dormitories, kitchens and dining halls go left and right of the
  axis or behind it. [26][28]
- **Flyers land, then walk.** In Zhu Xian, disciples fly to Tongtian Peak, land on the Cloud Sea
  plaza, cross the Rainbow Bridge and climb a wide stair to the Jade Pure Hall. In Renegade
  Immortal ch.9 a sword-flying disciple carries Wang Lin to the foot of the mountain, then they
  climb. [63][64][65][59]
- **Teleport arrays:** round stone or jade platforms tens of feet across, ringed by carved
  pillars (World of Xianxia ch.65: blue jade, five 10-foot pillars). Placed at the mountain foot
  beside the gate stele and on each peak's plaza, with a path leading off. [70][71][62]
- **Cave abodes** are doors cut into a cliff with a small ledge and railing, private and scattered
  (Mortal's Journey: you may open one after Foundation Establishment). Each still needs a stair,
  plank walk or private landing ledge. [61][77]
- **Floating islands** look believable because cloud hides their bases (Zhangjiajie inspired
  Avatar's Hallelujah Mountains). Give each an underside, a tether and a connector. [81][82][69]
- **Rhythm.** Alternate dense clusters with open bridges, platforms and plazas; keep the path
  always climbing or dropping. Long dense stretches feel oppressive. [54]
- **Goal sense.** Keep a landmark in view from most paths so every route leads somewhere. [54][57]
- **Signs of use** prove a path is a path: disciples sweeping steps, a kitchen next to the main
  plaza, rows of dormitories. [63][64][65]
- **Bridges and gates as defence.** The Rainbow Bridge is held as a chokepoint at Tongtian Peak;
  a protective formation surrounds the sect. [66][62]
- **The Renegade Immortal look** (the user's chosen style): weathered grey stone, aged dark timber,
  moss, restrained colour, natural daylight and valley mist, wide cinematic framing, rare magic
  glow. "Grounded" and "restrained" are partly an inference from the show's press, not a
  quote. [56][55][60]
- **Weak references for layout:** Soul Land (academies and cities), Swallowed Star (science
  fiction), Perfect World (wilderness landscapes). Use Renegade Immortal, Mortal's Journey, Zhu
  Xian, Wudang and the Hanging Temple instead. [83][84]
- **Genshin's Liyue** added plank roads and suspension bridges between its karst peaks so the
  space between them feels crossable. [80]

## F. Checks the script does not run yet

From the level-design skill [85][86][87][88] and the Blender environment skills [89][90][91].
Each could be added to `scripts/walkcheck.py` later.

- **Door approach angle.** Measure the angle between the arriving path's direction at its landing
  and the direction from the landing to the main door. Flag it above 45°. This catches paths that
  arrive beside a building instead of at its door.
- **Stair sizes.** List every riser and tread per stair. Pass: risers 0.15 to 0.18 m (up to 0.25 m
  for distant mountain stairs), treads 0.26 m or more, risers in one flight within 10% of each
  other, width 1.5 m or more, railings about 0.9 m on cliff sides. The 0.26 m tread and 10% limits
  are common building practice, not from the skills.
- **Landmark visibility along the camera path.** Every 5th frame, test whether the global landmark
  (moon and summit pavilion) and the next area's hero building are in view and not blocked (ray
  from the camera). Targets: global landmark in view on most frames (the skill says 70%; lower
  for downward shots); next hero building visible at least 2 s before arrival at 3% or more of
  frame height.
- **Blur test.** About once a second of flight, render a tiny frame (160x90, Workbench or EEVEE),
  blur it heavily, and check that the next destination or main door is in the brightest 20% and
  that brightness is not even across the frame.
- **Scale figures and headroom.** At least one 1.75 m figure in each hero frame. Nothing hangs
  within 2.3 m above the main route (eaves, beams, arches over stairs).
- **Metrics from the skill:** paths at least 1.5 m wide (2.5 times body width), doors at least
  2.3 m by 1.2 m, comfortable ceiling 2.7 m, railings about 0.9 m, comfortable hop 2.4 m across
  and 1.2 m up. Monumental doors per spec 3.5 to 4.5 m.
- **Grid.** Snap buildings, terraces and path ends to one grid: 4 m facade modules on a 1 m
  sub-grid, 0.5 m for interiors. Do not mix grid sizes.
- **Order of work.** Lock the paths in grey blocking before lighting, materials or prompts.
  "If the space doesn't work in graybox, lighting won't save it."

## G. Ming Sect connection plan, area by area

Derived from the area briefs in `blender/xianxia-sect/spec.md` and the connector code in
`sect/world.py`. Not checked against the current geometry: run `scripts/walkcheck.py` to see what
is actually broken. Flight order and connector type: gate, stairway, mirror, stairway, forge,
chain bridge, arena (floating), stepping stones, mission, stairway, library, stairway with an
arched bridge over a notch, steles, stairway, scroll, stairway, formation, stairway, abode,
stairway, summit.

1. **gate (Mountain Gate).** Arrival: foot of the grand marble stair in the clouds (local
   y ≈ −130). Route: stair with a landing about every 20 steps and paired stone lanterns, the top
   plaza, the paifang's centre bay (an archway, no door: the stair must aim at the centre bay),
   the plaza behind. Leaves by: the stairway to mirror from a landing in line with the centre bay.
   Watch: guardian beasts and sword monoliths flank the axis, never block it.
2. **mirror (Heart-Mirror Terrace).** Arrival: landing where the gate stairway arrives. Route:
   paved path, zig-zag bridge over the pond, hexagonal pavilion platform with steps on the bridge
   side. The bronze mirror stands on the pavilion's view line; either give it a small paved
   viewing terrace joined to the path, or keep it across the water as a view target that nobody
   walks to. Watch: the zig-zag bridge touches the shore paving and the pavilion platform at deck
   height.
3. **forge (Artifact Forge Peak).** Arrival: landing, then a forecourt in front of the open-fronted
   forge hall. Route: forecourt, hall stair, hall floor around the cauldron. Work yards (anvils,
   quench troughs, racks, ore) on paving joined to the forecourt. The 15 artifacts float on
   purpose. Leaves by: the chain bridge to the arena from a bridgehead landing; set its posts at
   least 2 m inside the terrace edge (now about 0.7 m).
4. **arena (Sword-Discussion Arena, floating).** Arrival: the chain bridge lands on a bridgehead
   terrace at the rim, in front of an archway into the dueling ring. Route: a rim walkway between
   the banner poles; the spectator tier has stairs down to the rim. Leaves by: a second rim
   landing toward mission. Watch: leave a clear gap in the banner ring at each arrival. Loose
   anchor chains are fine; nothing that looks like a walkway may end loose.
5. **mission (Mission Hall).** Arrival: today the stepping stones from the arena, about 1.7 m up
   per hop and ending about 1 m past the terrace. Replace with a chain or plank bridge, or stones
   at most 1.2 m up and 2.4 m apart that end on a landing. Route: landing, broad plaza, past the
   mission wall (the path goes round or in front, never blocked), moon terrace with three front
   flights, the hall's centre door. Bell and drum towers on small platforms with stairs facing
   the axis; side corridors join the towers and the hall at floor level.
6. **library (Scripture Pavilion).** Arrival: landing, a paved path along the lotus pool (a
   bridge across it if the pool lies between arrival and pagoda), the pagoda platform stair, the
   ground-floor door. Study pavilions on short branch paths. The scrolls float on purpose.
   Leaves by: the stairway to steles.
7. **steles (Stele Forest).** Arrival: the stairway with the arched bridge over the notch; both
   bridge ends continue as stair at deck height. Route: entrance gate on the axis, the central
   aisle (at least 12 m wide), the end pavilion over the central stele with steps on the aisle
   side. Covered corridors frame the courtyard and join the gate and the end pavilion at floor
   level. Terraces step up with stairs on the axis.
8. **scroll (Jade Slip Pavilion).** Arrival: landing, the terrace with the stone desk, the
   pavilion's front stair, the door. An outside stair or a visible inner stair for the upper
   storey. Balustrade along the cliff edge. The jade scroll and slips float on purpose.
9. **formation (Sect-Protecting Formation).** Arrival: the stairway lands at the plaza rim on the
   axis, ideally through a gate or archway. The 8 flag pillars stand on the plaza. The array core
   floats on purpose; the dome is see-through. Leaves by: the stairway to abode.
10. **abode (Cave Abode).** Arrival: landing on the plateau in front of the cliff. Route: a path,
    the ledge terrace, the moon-gate door in the cliff. If the door or balcony sits above plateau
    level, a rock-cut stair or plank walk leads up to it. Railing on the balcony. A path to the
    edge of the koi pool. Leaves by: the stairway to summit.
11. **summit (Moon-Gazing Summit).** Arrival: the last flight aims at the crescent-moon arch on the
    axis, placed at the top of the flight like Mount Tai's South Gate of Heaven. Route: the arch,
    the circular terrace, the round pavilion's platform stair. Railing on the terrace edge.

## H. Full phrase bank

Connection:
- the whole sect is one connected network of stone stairs, bridges and plazas; every building is reachable on foot
- every building visibly reached by a stair, bridge or walkway; paths continuous from the foreground to the summit
- every building is reached by a visible stone stairway or paved path
- each path leads straight to a hall's front door
- the path continues out of frame toward the next peak
- architecturally coherent, logically connected sect complex

Layout and axis:
- traditional Chinese mountain sect laid out along one central axis, a single continuous stone stairway climbing from the mountain gate to the summit hall
- axial temple layout: gate, forecourt, stair, main hall door aligned on one line
- formal halls aligned on a central axis, dormitories and kitchens along the sides
- a main peak in the centre with smaller side peaks around it, each linked to the main peak by a bridge
- main hall largest with double eaves and a hipped roof, side halls smaller with single eaves, all facing the open valley
- bell tower and drum tower just inside the gate, mirrored side halls facing each other across the courtyard
- Chinese mountain monastery architecture like Wudang, built into the cliffs

Platforms, stairs, terraces:
- each hall stands on a raised stone platform with a flight of stone steps centred on its front door
- a broad moon terrace in front of the main hall, with three flights of steps at its front
- paved forecourt in front of every hall entrance
- courtyards stepped up the slope on stone terraces held by retaining walls, joined by stairs on the central axis
- halls step up the mountainside on terraces with stone retaining walls, rooflines stacked one above another
- a grand weathered stone stairway climbs from the mountain gate archway up the cliff to the main plaza, broken by landings and small gate arches
- continuous white marble stairway linking terrace to terrace, both ends landing on paved forecourts
- the stairway passes through stone archways and gate towers at each landing, with a rest pavilion at every turn
- the top gate stands in the saddle between two peaks, the final stair aimed straight at it
- the summit hall enclosed by a stone wall with a gate at the top of a steep stair
- landings at the top and bottom of every stair flight
- stairs with many small even steps, consistent human scale
- a stair cut into the rock face zigzags up the cliff
- stone steps lined with carved inscriptions on the rock

Courtyards and corridors:
- a raised paved walkway down the middle of the courtyard from the gate to the hall steps
- red-pillared covered corridors run around the courtyard and connect the side halls to the main hall
- a stepped covered corridor climbs the hillside between two halls at different heights
- a three-doored mountain gate in a red enclosure wall, with a stone paifang archway and a spirit screen wall on the approach

Bridges and cliff walks:
- a stone arch bridge over a pond leading straight to the central gate opening
- a stone arch bridge with carved balustrades crosses the ravine
- bridges firmly anchored on cliff terraces at both ends
- an iron-chain suspension bridge with a wooden plank deck and chain handrails spans the gap, anchored in small stone bridgehead pavilions at both ends
- a roofed wooden corridor bridge links the two terraces
- a roofed timber covered bridge with benches and a small shrine spanning the gorge, each end landing on a stone terrace in front of a gateway
- a narrow wooden plank walkway pinned to the sheer cliff face with iron chains leads to the next ledge
- cliff halls on timber beams socketed into the rock, a one-person plank walkway with a railing running along the beam ends
- halls built on wooden beams set into the cliff under a rock overhang, like the Hanging Temple, joined by walkways
- two cliff towers joined by a narrow plank bridge between their top floors
- stone railings along every cliff-edge path and stair

Arrival, floating, caves:
- a wide open landing plaza at the cliff edge where cultivators arrive on flying swords and continue on foot
- each floating peak has a landing terrace at its edge where the bridge arrives in front of a gate
- a circular jade teleportation platform ringed by five carved stone pillars, a stair leading off it onto the main path
- the floating island is tethered to the neighbouring peak by heavy iron chains, with a chain bridge as its only walkway
- a rocky inverted underside with hanging roots beneath the floating island
- a cave dwelling carved into the cliff: a round moon-gate door, a small ledge terrace with a railing, stone steps leading up to it
- Zhangjiajie-style sandstone pillars rising from a sea of clouds

Guiding the eye and scale:
- the colossal moon and the summit pavilion visible in the distance as a guiding landmark
- the next peak's hero building visible ahead along the route
- stone lanterns lining the main path toward the lit hall door
- main hall door glowing warm, framed by red lacquered columns
- small human figures on the stairs and terraces for scale
- tiny figures of disciples climbing the stairs and crossing the bridge, for scale
- disciples sweeping the stone steps, stone lanterns lining the path
- clear foreground, midground and background layers, one focal building per view

Style (Renegade Immortal look):
- grounded realistic 3D donghua style in the manner of Renegade Immortal
- weathered grey stone, aged dark timber, moss in the cracks, natural soft daylight, mist drifting through the valleys
- restrained colours, cinematic wide composition, believable real-world construction

Chinese:
- 依山而建，层层台地，石阶相连 (built into the mountain, terrace above terrace, linked by stone stairs)
- 山门、石阶、主峰、侧峰、铁索桥、栈道、洞府、云海广场 (mountain gate, stone stairs, main peak, side peaks, iron-chain bridge, cliff plank road, cave abode, cloud-sea plaza)

Negative:
- isolated buildings with no path, stairs ending in mid-air, bridges ending at blank walls or roofs, doors with no steps, walkways that stop at a cliff edge
- no buildings without a door, terrace or path; no doors opening onto a drop
- no floating buildings without a foundation, no impossible or Escher-like geometry
- no glowing auras everywhere, no neon colours
