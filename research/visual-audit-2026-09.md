# Portfolio visual audit

Reviewed September 24–25, 2026. Live site: https://art3m1s.me/.

## Scope and evidence

Inspected the live page at 1440×900, 820×1180, 390×844, and 375×667; reviewed every major section, the Dispatch AI detail modal, an education flip, and category/year filter combinations. Cross-checked observations against the local source. No application code was changed. Voice calls, microphone permissions, external project destinations, and actual phone hardware were not tested. The mobile checks are viewport simulations, not device certification. The PDF observation is specific to the embedded browser used here.

Design judgments below describe the visual impression; they do not establish whether anything was actually generated by AI. Accessibility spot checks used the current Vercel Web Interface Guidelines: https://raw.githubusercontent.com/vercel-labs/web-interface-guidelines/main/command.md. This is not a complete accessibility conformance audit.

## Overall assessment

The site has a consistent palette but an inconsistent hierarchy and interaction system. Purple/pink gradients, glowing dots, rounded pills, thin borders, and italic serif emphasis recur almost everywhere. Meanwhile, the page changes from stacked sticky cards to a horizontal scroll gallery, moving award tickers, a timeline, flip cards, a word-reveal statement, a PDF viewer, technical grids, and a decorative personal section.

That combination creates the template-like impression: the visual treatments are highly repetitive, but the ways visitors have to read and operate the page keep changing. The work itself is more distinctive than its presentation. The strongest opportunities are to foreground actual product imagery and outcomes, shorten the reading path, and reserve motion and accent color for a few meaningful moments.

## Highest-priority corrections

### 1. The strongest content arrives too late

**Observed:** At 1440×900, projects begin about 3,014px down the document. About alone is 2,114px tall. Experience occupies about 2,943px; the personal section starts around 14,801px. At 390×844, the full document was roughly 18,075px tall. These measurements describe the inspected state, not fixed values across devices.

**Impact:** Visitors must work through a large introduction before seeing the work. Repeated claims about voice systems, hackathons, and prizes lengthen the page without adding equivalent evidence.

**Recommendation:** Put selected work directly after a shorter hero. Compress About into one concise block. Keep expanded career detail and the site's architecture available as optional reading. Move a small portrait or personal detail much earlier.

**Source:** `client/src/app/page.tsx:28`; `client/src/components/sections/about.tsx:42`; `client/src/components/sections/about.tsx:62`.

### 2. No useful persistent navigation

**Observed:** The fixed top bar contains a name badge and, on desktop, a clock. It has no Work, Experience, About, or Contact links. An index appears at the very bottom.

**Impact:** A very long page gives visitors little orientation or direct access to the sections they came for. The name badge looks like a conventional home control but is a span.

**Recommendation:** Add a restrained navigation row with Work, Experience, About, Contact, and Resume. Provide a compact mobile equivalent and make the name a home link. The clock should be secondary if retained.

**Source:** `client/src/components/topbar.tsx:32`.

### 3. The mobile project modal wastes its available width

**Observed:** At 390px wide, the modal has 32px outside padding on each side, then another 24px inside each side. The prose and video get roughly 260–275px of width after borders and scrollbar space. The story becomes a tall, narrow column. The floating microphone overlaps the lower-right reading area.

**Impact:** Opening the most important content makes it harder to read. The nested scrolling window feels cramped.

**Recommendation:** Use a full-width mobile sheet or dedicated project page, with 20px content padding, a clear close header, and enough room for media. Collapse or reposition the microphone while reading project details.

**Source:** `client/src/components/sections/projects/project-detail.tsx:63`, `:68`, `:80`; `client/src/components/voice-orb/index.tsx:727`.

### 4. Project badges visibly overlap

**Observed:** On Dispatch AI, the revealed “deep dive” badge occupies the same upper-right area as the long award pill. This was visible in both desktop and narrow viewport captures when the card was hovered/focused.

**Impact:** Text collides at precisely the moment the visitor is considering opening the project.

**Recommendation:** Put the award in a separate metadata row below the image. Use a stable “View project” link near the title, or reserve a separate corner for the action. Do not rely on hover to explain what a card does.

**Source:** `client/src/components/sections/projects/project-card.tsx:58`, `:67`.

### 5. Filter styling hides an active constraint

**Observed:** Selecting Safety and then 2025 shows SentinelAI, but only 2025 looks selected. Fintech then 2025 similarly shows Vocalyze while Fintech loses its active styling. Both filters continue to apply. Filter buttons also omit `aria-pressed`.

**Impact:** Visitors cannot tell why other projects are missing. The visual state contradicts the actual filtering state.

**Recommendation:** Show both selected filters. Separate category and year controls, add an obvious reset, and expose selection programmatically. Label the result explicitly, such as “1 project · Safety · 2025.”

**Source:** `client/src/components/sections/projects/filter-bar.tsx:44`; `client/src/components/sections/projects/index.tsx:228`.

### 6. Small text and bright gradients reduce legibility

**Observed:** Dates, captions, metadata, and flip labels commonly use 10.5–13px text with `--muted`. Token contrast calculates to approximately 3.80:1 against the base background and 3.50:1 against the card background. White against the gradient's pink midpoint is about 2.46:1. These are solid-color/token calculations, not measurements of every composited pixel; they explain the visibly faint metadata and weak white text on bright gradient buttons.

**Recommendation:** Raise metadata brightness and size. Use a solid, sufficiently dark action color with light text. Treat gradient fills as decoration unless the entire text-bearing area has adequate contrast. Recheck actual composited backgrounds after changes.

**Source:** `client/src/app/globals.css:12`; `client/src/components/sections/education.tsx:98`; `client/src/components/sections/projects/filter-bar.tsx:16`; `client/src/components/sections/resume.tsx:81`.

### 7. Mobile gallery pagination is too small to operate comfortably

**Observed:** The active dot measured 24×8px; inactive dots are styled 8×8px. There is no larger padded button area.

**Impact:** Swiping works as an alternative, but the visible direct controls are unnecessarily hard to tap.

**Recommendation:** Keep the small visual dots inside buttons with generous hit areas, or use accessible previous/next buttons with a numeric count. Aim for approximately 44px touch areas.

**Source:** `client/src/components/sections/projects/index.tsx:519`.

## Design and content hierarchy

### 8. Almost every heading uses the same dramatic formula

**Observed:** “A builder who listens first,” “Things I shipped,” “Thirty-six wins,” “A short, productive career,” “Three schools, one path,” “The printable version,” and “A few true things” repeat large sans text with a purple italic serif phrase. Most section headings can reach 96px.

**Impact:** Secondary sections receive nearly the same visual emphasis as the main introduction. After a few sections the treatment feels automatic.

**Recommendation:** Keep one expressive headline treatment. Use simpler, smaller section headings afterward. Reserve serif emphasis for a genuinely special quotation or personal moment. Establish clear roles for display, section title, project title, body, and metadata.

**Source:** `client/src/components/sections/hero.tsx:108`; `client/src/components/sections/education.tsx:49`; `client/src/components/sections/resume.tsx:28`.

### 9. Too many surfaces compete for attention

**Observed:** Section labels, navigation-like prompts, filters, award badges, status indicators, tags, and the name all use pill or badge treatments. Most cards combine borders, purple surfaces, gradients, and glow.

**Impact:** Decoration stops communicating importance. Static labels can look actionable, and primary actions are less distinctive.

**Recommendation:** Use plain text for section labels and metadata, one selected-control treatment, and one primary action treatment. Let most sections sit directly on the page. Retain containers where they meaningfully group content.

**Source:** `client/src/app/globals.css:3`; `client/src/components/sections/about.tsx:45`; `client/src/components/sections/projects/project-card.tsx:38`.

### 10. The hero sells interaction before showing professional identity

**Observed:** “Ask me anything” dominates; the role and employer appear in the paragraph. Resume is the primary filled button. Voice is a distant floating microphone, while five chat-like prompts actually navigate or filter instead of asking a question.

**Impact:** A visitor has to infer the intended first action. The presentation resembles an AI product landing page more than an introduction to a specific engineer.

**Recommendation:** Give the name, specialty, and strongest work more prominence. Put a clearly labeled voice demonstration near the copy, alongside an obvious route to selected work. Present section shortcuts as navigation; if prompts remain conversational, make their behavior explicit.

**Source:** `client/src/components/sections/hero.tsx:12`, `:57`, `:108`; `client/src/components/voice-orb/index.tsx:430`.

### 11. Project thumbnails are inconsistent and often fail to show the product

**Observed:** The gallery mixes a dispatcher interface, an AdaptEd frame labeled KUMO with a speaker inset, a TalkTuahBank statistics slide, and other presentation/video frames. Overlays dim these further. At tablet size, Vocalyze shows a “Pitch Deck” title slide.

**Impact:** The visuals feel assembled from available video thumbnails rather than deliberately selected. Some images do not clearly connect to the displayed project name or demonstrate what was built.

**Recommendation:** Select one representative product screenshot per project, use consistent framing and image proportions, and retain natural colors. Put outcomes and awards outside the image. Include a clear fallback for projects without useful product imagery.

**Source:** `client/src/components/sections/projects/project-card.tsx:41`.

### 12. The desktop gallery changes the meaning of scrolling

**Observed:** Vertical page scrolling moves project cards horizontally inside a pinned viewport. The wrapper has a minimum height of 220vh even when filtering leaves one project. At 820×1180, the one-result section still measured 2,596px high. Tablet layout uses the desktop rail with a compressed heading/filter area.

**Impact:** Visitors cannot skim projects in a conventional reading flow. A single result still consumes more than two viewport heights without additional content.

**Recommendation:** Use a normal responsive grid for the archive and a clear featured project layout. If a rail remains, make it directly scrollable and provide explicit controls. Remove artificial travel distance for one-result states and use the simpler layout on tablets.

**Source:** `client/src/components/sections/projects/index.tsx:244`, `:543`; `client/src/hooks/use-media-query.ts:23`.

### 13. About cards devote too much space to short text

**Observed:** Four wide cards have a minimum height of 360px on desktop, while their body copy is limited to 680px. Large empty right-hand areas and repeated glows dominate. Sticky stacking also makes comparing paragraphs less natural.

**Recommendation:** Combine the introduction into two or three compact paragraphs with selected proof points. If cards are retained, remove forced heights and use their remaining space for relevant evidence or imagery.

**Source:** `client/src/components/sections/about.tsx:62`.

### 14. Awards are presented as moving decoration

**Observed:** Two continuously moving rows contain event names, years, and award categories. They clip at the edges, have no visible pause control, and do not provide a stable list to scan.

**Impact:** Important evidence is harder to inspect than ordinary body text. Visitors must wait for items to pass.

**Recommendation:** Show three or four major awards in a stationary summary and offer a complete readable list. If a decorative ticker remains, provide pause behavior and keep it separate from the primary evidence.

**Source:** `client/src/components/sections/hackathons/hackathons-section.tsx:57`; `client/src/app/globals.css:53`.

### 15. Education cards give minor content disproportionate weight

**Observed:** Each card is 460px tall. On mobile the three cards stack, consuming 1,380px before gaps and section framing. High school receives the same size as the master's degree. Flipping USC reveals only a brief sentence beneath the repeated degree heading. Logos also have uneven apparent size within their white tiles.

**Recommendation:** Use compact education rows. Present the relevant notes directly and de-emphasize older education. Normalize logo visual size rather than only their outer containers.

**Source:** `client/src/components/sections/education.tsx:72`, `:82`.

### 16. The skills statement is long to scroll and initially difficult to read

**Observed:** A single sentence occupies a 155vh section on desktop and 130vh on phones. Unrevealed words are at 18% opacity. On entry, only the beginning is readily legible.

**Impact:** It adds another large interruption and makes comprehension depend on scrolling. It repeats the site's existing positioning instead of supplying a useful skill summary.

**Recommendation:** Show the entire statement at readable contrast in a compact area, or replace it with a short capability summary supported by project links. Motion can decorate the statement without withholding its text.

**Source:** `client/src/components/sections/skills.tsx:30`, `:39`.

### 17. Resume preview can become an unexplained blank block

**Observed:** The embedded PDF remained a gray panel in this browser on desktop and mobile. Open/download controls exist, but mobile still reserves a 440px preview area. This does not establish that the PDF itself is broken.

**Recommendation:** Provide a reliable image preview linked to the PDF, or a compact download card. Avoid reserving a large empty area when an embedded viewer is unavailable. Keep file actions immediately visible.

**Source:** `client/src/components/sections/resume.tsx:43`, `:48`.

### 18. Typography changes role across sections

**Observed:** Project card titles use a bold sans face, their modal titles switch to serif gradient text, technical architecture labels use serif, award metrics use bold sans, and repeated resume metrics use thin serif. Bodies and metadata also vary across many fractional sizes.

**Impact:** Matching content types do not always look related. The resume and architecture sections feel like separate design kits inside the same color theme.

**Recommendation:** Define one title style per content role, one body scale, and one metadata scale. Use the same project-title treatment in summary and detail views; use one metric style across sections.

**Source:** `client/src/components/sections/projects/project-card.tsx:73`; `client/src/components/sections/projects/project-detail.tsx:85`; `client/src/components/sections/architecture.tsx:129`; `client/src/components/sections/resume.tsx:99`.

### 19. Personal content is overdecorated and buried

**Observed:** The portrait is the first substantial human image, late in the page. Facts are individually boxed with star icons, while oversized low-opacity phrases run behind the content. Several facts repeat career and AI positioning under “Off the clock.”

**Recommendation:** Introduce the portrait earlier. Present personal facts as a short editorial passage or simple list. Remove the background text that intersects the reading area. Let actual hobbies, photographs, and specific details provide the personality.

**Source:** `client/src/components/sections/personal/personal-section.tsx:53`, `:56`, `:83`.

### 20. Contact is overshadowed by its headline

**Observed:** “LET'S talk. (LITERALLY.)” fills a large part of the desktop footer, while the email is much smaller below. Copy characterizes email as “old-school.” The “Tap voice orb” link actually scrolls to the hero instead of opening the voice interface.

**Impact:** The most conventional contact action is visually and verbally de-emphasized. The voice link's behavior does not match its label.

**Recommendation:** Make email the clear contact action, retain optional voice beside it, shorten the headline, and either open the voice panel from the link or rename it to describe its destination.

**Source:** `client/src/components/sections/personal/footer.tsx:28`, `:51`.

## Motion and consistency follow-up

- The global CSS handles reduced animation durations, and the hero explicitly gates its JavaScript motion. However, the personal-section parallax and skills reveal do not use that same preference guard. The project rail also retains its scroll-driven translation. These are source-review findings; reduced-motion behavior was not exercised in the browser.
- The custom cursor replaces native cursor feedback above 900px. Its initialization checks width only once, while CSS reacts to resizing. A page loaded narrow and widened later can hide the native cursor without installing the custom movement handler. Prefer the native cursor; if retaining the custom one, respond to pointer capability and breakpoint changes.
- Main containers mix a 1280px maximum with fixed padding and other sections using 8vw padding plus a 1240px inner maximum. The difference is modest at the reviewed desktop size but will widen on larger displays. Adopt a shared content container and a small spacing scale.
- A consistent dark palette and responsive stacking already exist. Project modal close behavior and education buttons are implemented as real controls, and the project modal has focus handling. Preserve these useful foundations while simplifying the presentation.

## Suggested order of work

1. Correct badge overlap, hidden filter selection, contrast, small touch targets, and mobile modal/widget collision.
2. Add navigation and move selected work immediately below the hero.
3. Replace the pinned project rail with an easily scanned layout; remove excess section heights and compress repeated material.
4. Standardize headings, metadata, buttons, containers, and image framing.
5. Reduce repeated glows, gradients, pills, and background lettering; bring real screenshots and personal imagery forward.
6. Recheck at phone, tablet, laptop, and wide desktop sizes, including keyboard navigation, reduced motion, project details, combined filters, and PDF fallback.

The intended result is a portfolio whose first impression comes from Bill's work, judgment, and personality, with the voice interaction serving as one memorable demonstration.
